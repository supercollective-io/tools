# Relationship findings — 4 November 2026

## Decision

Do not advance this relationship format. All four eligible compositions completed
and remained coherent, but the added edges provided no clear improvement across
the two matched snapshots. They added roughly eleven seconds of preparation and
10–12% composer input. Two of seven selected relationships also require correction
at source level. These findings concern this small reader-generated edge format,
not GraphRAG or graph representations in general.

The unfinished-snapshot edge condition used a concrete dry-bench detail that its
node-only counterpart did not select. That is an interesting outcome to retain,
but one phrase does not establish a reliable relationship benefit. The developed
pair made very similar connections with and without edges.

## Relationship support and resulting writing

Five edges have a defensible textual basis. Examples include the dry end of Ari's
bench as a concrete instance of knowing where to sit, and Bea and Dev both resisting
a universal preference for being noticed. Those are reader interpretations of
related writing, not evidence that the participants explicitly agreed.

Two edges are problematic:

- In the unfinished snapshot, Bea's statement that recognition is not always
  comforting is labelled a **qualification** of liking being one face among many.
  It more naturally reinforces or explains that preference; the quotes do not
  supply a limitation on it. The edge type can mislead a composer about the
  relationship, even though the endpoint quotes are exact.
- Another edge says that not having to explain one's presence **supports the
  ability to stay without buying anything**. Chen provides both facts, but does
  not establish that the former enables the latter. A shared theme of being free
  from demands would be a more defensible interpretation than that directional
  claim. The edge also introduces an unsupported feminine pronoun for Chen.

These outputs were retained unchanged. Structural validation correctly accepted
valid endpoints and syntax; it cannot establish semantic support.

The unfinished edge composition connects knowing which end of a bench stays dry
with being able to sit in the library without purchase or explanation. Its
node-only counterpart makes the broader connection between freedom from having
to explain oneself and discomfort with recognition. Both thoughts are grounded.
The developed pair both connect the library's undemanding space with the option
to talk without obligation. The node-only version additionally states that being
noticed is not always comforting. Neither pair establishes that edges improved
an individual's experience of representation or conversation.

No composition reproduces the problematic causal edge as a causal statement.
All four preserve the exact prefix and a coherent join. They neither complete the
unfinished `wel` nor claim that everybody welcomes recognition. Selective omission
remains expected; a missing person or edge is not automatically an artistic
failure.

An anonymised Luna assessment of the compositions is saved separately from the
primary edge audit. It saw the available source snapshots and texts, not method
labels or packets. This is external fixed-script evidence; the two individual
experiential goals still require responsive AI-participant exchanges in confirmation.

Continue to diverse original excerpts, followed separately by the three summary
