# Criteria for assessing the collective voice

Working proposal recorded on 5 November 2026, following discussion with the
creator and two GPT-6 Luna subagent critiques. The creator requested that this
proposal be documented. It is a basis for developing assessment, not a validated
scale, a final scoring rubric or a new set of automated acceptance rules.

The central questions are whether people can find themselves in the voice,
whether it does justice to what they contributed, and whether something worth
encountering emerges between them. These are different achievements. A
comforting generality, an accurate summary and a compelling collective utterance
could each succeed at one while failing at the others.

This proposal builds on the [piece brief](rehearsing-we.md) and
[recognition and representation research](recognition-and-representation.md).
It gives artistic composition and performative quality explicit attention
alongside participant experience and faithfulness. Earlier
[composer assessments](composer-performance-assessment.md) retain their
historical protocols and results; this proposal does not retrospectively change
their assessment conditions.

## Candidate criteria and their proposed roles

| Criterion                       | What to assess                                                                                                                                                | Proposed role                                                        |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Recognition                     | Can someone find something reflecting their thinking, experience or concern? This could happen through another person's words.                                | Core                                                                 |
| Faithfulness                    | Does the voice preserve important meaning, uncertainty, conditions and stakes when it draws on a contribution?                                                | Core                                                                 |
| Plurality and belonging         | Can someone encounter positions they reject while still having a place in the collective? Are differences intelligible without becoming everybody's position? | Core                                                                 |
| Collective composition          | Do contributions acquire meaningful relationships, producing something beyond a catalogue of individual statements?                                           | Core                                                                 |
| Performative quality            | Is the voice compelling and possible to follow in the actual performance, through its language, rhythm, images, humour, tension or silence?                   | Core, assessed in context                                            |
| Generativity                    | Does it open a thought, question or possibility that someone could develop, resist or reconsider?                                                             | Exploratory dimension; avoid compulsory novelty                      |
| Distribution of attention       | Whose concerns shape the voice, and whose repeatedly disappear? Does eloquence, repetition or familiarity dominate selection?                                 | Essential diagnostic                                                 |
| Dependence on the contributions | Is this recognisably a response to these particular contributions, rather than a reusable passage?                                                            | Essential diagnostic                                                 |
| Responsiveness over time        | Can later contributions change the developing voice's direction or framing?                                                                                   | Core for the later conversational test                               |
| Collective agency               | Does the experience help people discover something they could understand or do together?                                                                      | Wider performance question; premature as a requirement for each text |
| Delivery and efficiency         | Is it timely, reliable and affordable enough to sustain the experience?                                                                                       | Assess separately from the voice's quality                           |

Core means something to examine explicitly, not something to maximise
independently. A diagnostic helps explain an outcome; it is not a quota or an
automatic pass condition.

## The five core criteria

### Recognition

Recognition concerns meaning, not merely subject matter. “My topic appeared” is
weaker than “that captures something I was trying to say”. Ask about recognition
separately from liking, agreement and endorsement.

Recognition need not be exclusive: several people might recognise themselves in
the same sentence. Nor must someone recognise their original wording. A person
can find their thinking expressed through someone else's contribution.

Recognition alone does not establish that the text was grounded in what the
person contributed. Conversely, an accurate condensation can fail to make them
feel recognised. Keep both findings available rather than allowing one to
overrule the other.

### Faithfulness

Preserve consequential distinctions while allowing transformation. The voice can
condense, juxtapose, interpret and invent connections; otherwise the assessment
risks rewarding only summaries. But it should not silently convert uncertainty
into conviction, a wish into a commitment, or an objection into support.

Grounding need not mean concrete anecdotes. Someone's broad concern about
fascism, climate or loneliness deserves representation at the level they
expressed it. Faithfulness concerns expressed meaning, not whether every factual
or political claim in a contribution is objectively true.

Omission alone does not establish misrepresentation. Nevertheless, selective
omission can give a misleading picture of the group, even when each retained
statement is accurate. Inspect both the meaning of material used and the
collective picture produced by selection.

### Plurality and belonging

Allow disagreement without expelling people from the “we”. This is more demanding
than inserting “some of us” occasionally. Conflicting positions should remain
intelligible, and the collective's framing should not assign everyone an
agreement they did not express.

Plurality does not require symmetrical treatment. A disagreement involving
unequal burdens should not automatically become two equally weighted
preferences. Nor does belonging require someone to endorse the collective's
words or adopt a shared identity.

### Collective composition

The collective itself speaks as “we”. This is the speaking perspective, not a
claim that every participant endorses every sentence. Differences and uncertainty
belong within that voice; splitting the group into individual speakers is not the
default way to preserve them. Assess whether a passage erases or distorts a
consequential difference, rather than treating first-person plural language alone
as evidence of invented consensus.

Creator clarification, 6 November 2026: the spoken voice should not explicitly
refer to “contributions” or narrate the composition process. Use “we” as the
ordinary speaking perspective. The initial preference for “part of us” over
“one of us” was subsequently qualified: “we resist” can be poetically stronger
than “part of us resists”. Qualify where a consequential distinction needs it,
without routinely dividing the collective into speakers. Enter the substance
directly rather than announcing “our first thought” or the stages of thinking.
Preserve the scope of personal facts; replacing a pronoun must not turn a
biography into shared experience.

Subsequent passage-level feedback favours connections made directly through the
speech over classificatory insertions, reportorial descriptions and generic
disclaimers about difference or insufficient hope. A qualification should preserve
a consequential meaning, rather than merely announce that the composer is being
careful. These are contextual artistic judgements, not blanket bans on particular
words or permission to erase real uncertainty. A question about collective
confidence can be productive while a definite assertion about its history can
misrepresent an explicitly uncertain source.

Composition is an artistic achievement in its own right. The voice should do
something with the material: reveal a connection, put experiences into tension,
develop an image or make a contradiction palpable.

Allow unresolved, awkward or surprising composition. Coherence means that we can
follow what is happening; it need not mean one stable position or a satisfying
conclusion. A new relationship can be valuable without having been stated by
any individual contributor.

### Performative quality

Assess the encounter, not literary polish. A beautiful paragraph can be
impossible to absorb in the room. A plain sentence can stop everyone.

Examine what holds attention and makes the collective perceptible under the
actual delivery conditions. Language, pace, rhythm, images, humour, tension and
silence are possibilities, not mandatory ingredients. Leave room for different
aesthetics rather than rewarding one polished model voice. A reading of the text
can suggest potential; it cannot establish how the performance lands.

## Example: preserving a difference while composing a relationship

These contributions are invented illustrations, not participant evidence:

> “I want shorter working hours.”
>
> “I can barely live on my current pay.”

“We want to work less” loses the second person's constraint. Listing both
faithfully achieves more, but little composition.

“We want time back, but some of us cannot afford to sell less of it” creates a
relationship while preserving a consequential difference. It is an interpretive
candidate, not a certified faithful answer: assess whether the relationship does
justice to the second person's concern about income, rather than rejecting its
collective “we” for lack of explicit unanimous endorsement. Whether the people
recognise themselves in it remains a separate question. The example shows why composition,
faithfulness and recognition require separate judgements.

## What should not define success

- **Consensus, approval or agreement.** These may occur, but convergence is not
  the definition of success.
- **Equal mentions or exhaustive coverage.** They reward inventories. Persistent
  exclusion still matters, and selective omission can misrepresent the group.
- **A required quantity of disagreement or novelty.** These encourage
  manufactured conflict and strained connections.
- **Compulsory hope, constructive solutions or actionability.** Anger, refusal,
  uncertainty and unresolved wishes belong in the performance.
- **Smoothness, brevity or a unified tone as automatic virtues.** Each can help,
  but each can also erase something important.
- **Immediate uptake of every correction or contribution.** Artistic selection
  is permitted; the collective text is not a record of commitments.
- **Political neutrality or agreement with the assessors' preferred politics.**
  Assess how power and disagreement are represented without requiring
  participants to reach the assessors' conclusions.
- **One combined quality score.** Strong rhythm should not cancel out serious
  distortion, and a high average should not conceal a consistently excluded
  perspective.

## Evidence for the next assessment

For each core criterion, record a judgement, a specific passage and the reason
it matters. Include contrary examples and unresolved interpretations. Keep
participant accounts alongside source comparisons and artistic readings rather
than allowing one to substitute for another.

| Evidence                                                | What it can establish                                                            | Limit                                                                                                                              |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A participant's account, anchored to particular moments | Their reported recognition, disagreement, belonging or experience of the voice   | Does not establish everyone else's experience or source correspondence by itself                                                   |
| Comparison of contributions with the collective text    | Preserved meaning, distortion, unsupported attribution and patterns of selection | Does not establish how a participant feels                                                                                         |
| Artistic reading or observation of the performance      | Composition, intelligibility, rhythm and effects in the observed conditions      | A text-only reading cannot establish the experience of the room                                                                    |
| Responsive AI-participant rehearsal                     | Simulated assessments and evidence of how an exchange develops                   | Must account for leading prompts, automatic agreement and shared model biases; transfer to human participants remains to be tested |

The current static test uses fictional contributions considered as complete
texts. Recognition judgements about those contributors remain simulated or
inferred; they are not reports from actual contributors. Static material can
support assessment of representation and conversational possibilities, but
cannot establish a responsive conversation. Add responsiveness over time when
participants can encounter the voice, answer it and influence its development.

Use the fixed [recognition control](recognition-and-representation.md#current-author-written-control)
to investigate how much recognition can occur without access to the test
contributions. Follow its recorded provenance and comparison conditions; it is
not guaranteed to represent nobody. A favourable response to it does not
invalidate someone's experience.

Also compare outputs from substantively different contribution sets: does the
voice change meaningfully? This probes dependence on the material without
requiring every individual input to visibly change the output. Differences in
style and ordinary generation variability remain possible explanations to
inspect, rather than attributing every change to the contributions.

Inspect individual experiences and patterns of exclusion alongside the overall
reading. Avoid reducing the assessment to average approval, mention counts or
an aggregate score. Report technical delivery and resource use separately.

## Artistic freedom and attribution

The most consequential open choice is how far the collective may go beyond what
anyone individually said. The proposed direction is considerable freedom to
create relationships and possibilities, with much stricter limits on
attributing beliefs, experiences or agreement to the people involved.

The next assessment should explore that boundary rather than presupposing that
either faithful condensation or maximal novelty is the ideal.
