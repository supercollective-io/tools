# Recognition and representation in a collective voice

Research recorded on 5 November 2026. **Evaluation criteria remain unsettled.**
The questions below are working possibilities, not accepted requirements, a
validated scale or a scoring rubric. This research supports the static
representation work described in the [piece brief](rehearsing-we.md) and
[corpus design](representation-corpus.md).

The subsequent [criteria proposal](collective-voice-criteria.md) develops this
research into five core dimensions, supporting diagnostics, exclusions and an
assessment approach. The questions below record the earlier exploration; the
proposal remains distinct from a validated scale or final scoring rubric.

## The distinction to preserve

**Recognition** is the experience of finding something of oneself in a text.
**Grounded representation** concerns whether the text faithfully carries what
these particular contributors expressed, including the scope of their agreement
and disagreement. Recognition is valuable, but does not by itself establish
representation. Faithfulness here concerns expressed meaning, not whether every
political or factual claim in a contribution is objectively true.

A general statement can genuinely apply to someone without having been derived
from their contribution. Conversely, an accurate condensation may fail to make
someone feel recognised. Neither a feeling nor a source-text audit substitutes
for the other. Shared language need not be unique to one contributor, and exact
quotation is not a requirement for recognition.

The creator's current intuition is to begin with each person's experience:
can they find something reflecting their thinking, and can they encounter
positions they reject while recognising those as parts of a complex collective,
rather than the position of the whole? This is the direction for further design,
not a settled formulation of success.

## Research and its limits

### Feeling heard and disagreement

Roos, Postmes and Koudenburg (2023), _Feeling heard: Operationalizing a key
concept for social relations_, developed an eight-item measure in surveys of
194 and 1,000 people. Their conceptual account combines voice, attention,
empathy, respect and mutual understanding; empirically, feeling heard emerged
as a unitary construct. An item about differing perspectives did not fit well,
suggesting disagreement and feeling unheard are not equivalent. The measure
concerns conversations, not recognition inside an authored collective text.
Adapting its questions would require new validation.
[Primary article](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0292865).

Ren and Schaumberg (2024), _Disagreement Gets Mistaken for Bad Listening_, report
11 studies involving 3,396 adults. Disagreeing listeners were judged worse at
listening even when listening behaviour was held constant. Acknowledgement
could improve perceptions but also imply agreement. This complicates the design:
understanding a position must be distinguishable from endorsing it. The studies
concern interpersonal listening, not the effectiveness of particular plural
forms of collective writing.
[Primary article](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/09567976241239935).

### Recognition without individual grounding

Forer (1949), _The fallacy of personal validation_, gave students the same general
personality sketch presented as individually derived feedback. They rated it
highly. Such acceptance does not establish that a process has discriminated
between individuals. The description can contain genuinely applicable statements;
recognition need not be an illusion for it to be insufficient evidence of
personalisation. This was a classroom personality-feedback demonstration, not a
study of collective text.
[Primary paper DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0059240),
[scan of the original](https://criticalthinkingtext.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/04/forer_the-fallacy-of-personal-validation_1949.pdf).

Snyder (1974), _Why horoscopes are true: The effects of specificity on acceptance
of astrological interpretations_, examined how purported specificity of birth
information affected acceptance. The finding concerns the framing of the reading,
not evidence that astrological interpretation is accurate. It motivates keeping
claims of personalisation separate from the text's actual grounding.
[Primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1002/1097-4679(197410)30:4%3C577::AID-JCLP2270300434%3E3.0.CO;2-8).

### AI responses and source expectations

Yin, Jia and Wakslak (2024), _AI can help people feel heard, but an AI label
diminishes this impact_, found AI-generated supportive messages could produce
stronger feelings of being heard than human-generated messages, while belief
that a message came from AI reduced that feeling. The study concerns personal
supportive responses, not faithful compression of a group's contributions.
Message quality, source expectations and input correspondence should therefore
be distinguished in our comparisons.
[Primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2319112121).

### Collective approval and dissent

Tessler and colleagues (2024), _AI can help humans find common ground in
democratic deliberation_, studied AI-mediated group statements using opinions
and critiques from 5,734 participants across studies. Participants preferred
AI-written statements to human mediators' statements. Views often converged,
and the researchers found incorporation of dissenting voices alongside majority
positions. This is a relevant precedent, but approval and convergence are not
our definition of successful representation. A popular collective passage could
still omit something important to a particular contributor.
[Authors' publication record and abstract](https://deepmind.google/research/publications/65220/),
[primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adq2852).

These studies establish neither a complete theory of artistic representation nor
a reliable recipe for making all contributors feel seen. The project-specific
inferences below need participant testing.

## Questions still under development

The earlier proposed categories were recognition, faithfulness, room for
difference, continued belonging and limits on speaking for someone. They are
retained here as discussion material only; the creator has not adopted them.
Possible questions include:

- What, if anything, reflects what you meant? Can you point to it?
- Is your subject mentioned while something important about your meaning is lost?
- Does a passage you disagree with sound like one perspective or everybody's view?
- Does your own perspective still have a place alongside that disagreement?
- Has the text made you more certain, committed or agreeable than you intended?

Unresolved issues include how much of a contribution must survive condensation,
whether recognition can arise through someone else's words, how people interpret
an unqualified collective “we”, and how to distinguish liking a new idea from
having one's earlier contribution represented. Do not collapse these into one
recognition or representation score before deciding what that score would mean.
Omission alone does not settle whether someone is misrepresented.

## Candidate comparison methods

These are design proposals, not empirically validated interventions:

1. Collect an unprompted response to the collective text before asking people to
   highlight recognition, mismatch or disagreement. Avoid teaching the desired
   reaction through the evaluation questions.
2. Compare highlights with the source contribution, and ask the contributor about
   ambiguous meanings. Keep interpretation, direct support and newly introduced
   material distinguishable. An independent reader can help audit correspondence,
   but cannot replace the participant's account of their meaning.
3. Compare a grounded output with a fixed, input-independent recognition control.
   Match presentation, approximate length and delivery; counterbalance order.
   A difference may still reflect style or specificity, not grounding alone.
4. Inspect individual experiences and minority positions, not just average liking
   or endorsement. Agreement with the text and recognition of oneself should be
   asked about separately.

A fixed text scoring highly does not prove deception or invalidate someone's
experience. It shows why recognition alone cannot validate the representation
process. A grounded text doing poorly could indicate missing meaning, poor
expression, misunderstanding, or disagreement; investigate rather than assuming.

### How the control seeks recognition

| Writing choice                                             | Intended effect and evidential status                                                                                               |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Broad wishes for a kinder, fairer life                     | High applicability leaves many readers a point of entry; informed by general-feedback research, not proven for this passage.        |
| Wanting change while fearing its costs                     | Opposing tendencies give readers multiple points of identification; a writing hypothesis rather than a measured independent effect. |
| Describing hesitation as care and thoughtfulness           | Offers a favourable explanation of difficulty; may increase liking, which must not be confused with fidelity.                       |
| Moving between family, work, community and the wider world | Allows personal or political interpretations without selecting a cohort's actual concern.                                           |
| Recognising both influence and limits                      | Accommodates agency and frustration without requiring a specific commitment.                                                        |
| A modest hopeful ending                                    | Makes the text emotionally coherent; it might improve approval rather than recognition.                                             |

The aim is high subjective recognition, not a claim that this wording maximises
it. No audience has evaluated the passage. It deliberately lacks evidence of
which people want which changes, their reasons or their actual disagreements.
Its broad tensions can resemble plural representation without establishing it.
The control supplies no fake evidence that it heard participants, no invented
biography, and no claim of personal tailoring.

### Use in a comparison

Introduce the study truthfully as comparing texts that may be prewritten or based
on contributions. Decide the disclosure procedure before testing; do not falsely
label this passage as derived from what the audience said. Source labels can
change the experience and should not be allowed to confound an otherwise textual
comparison. Explain the fixed control's role afterwards if its identity was not
revealed beforehand.

Keep the control independent of each contribution set. Use the same display or
voice, similar duration, and equivalent reading opportunities for all conditions.
Record recognition, liking, perceived agreement and actual input correspondence
separately while the final criteria remain open. Real semantic overlap with a
cohort is possible and should be acknowledged. This control tests what recognition
can occur without access to the inputs; it is not guaranteed to represent nobody.
