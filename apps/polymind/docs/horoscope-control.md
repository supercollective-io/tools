# Writing the fixed horoscope control

Recorded on 5 November 2026. The current author-written passage is
[`recognition-control-v2.txt`](../examples/recognition-control-v2.txt): 149 words,
approximately one spoken minute, not yet timed or evaluated. The main assistant
wrote v2 directly at the creator's request; subagents supplied research and planning,
not its prose. The [earlier subagent draft](../examples/recognition-control-v1.txt)
remains unchanged for provenance.

The purpose is a demanding comparison for the Composer: can a fixed passage make
people recognise themselves without responding to their actual contributions?
It answers “What is something you’d like to change, but can’t change on your own?”
in a collective “we”. It is not an astrological prediction or a personality test.
[Recognition and representation](recognition-and-representation.md) remain
separate questions, and the project's evaluation criteria remain unsettled.

## Research used in writing

### General applicability and subjective validation

Forer (1949) supplied the same general personality sketch to students who believed
they were receiving individually derived feedback. Their high ratings showed why
acceptance alone cannot validate an individual assessment. Broad statements can
truly apply to many people while contributing little evidence of discrimination
between them. This motivates a fixed text that leaves room for readers to supply
their own concrete referents; it does not establish that v2 will succeed.
[Original paper](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0059240).

### The expectation of personalisation

Snyder (1974) gave an identical horoscope to 63 people across three conditions.
It was described as generally applicable, derived from birth year and month, or
derived from birth year, month and day. Mean acceptance increased across those
conditions (3.24, 3.76 and 4.38 on a five-point scale). The manipulated variable was
purported derivation, not more specific wording. Therefore this study informs our
handling of source labels; it does not prove that adding detailed-sounding phrases
makes the text more effective. The study used a small all-female sample recruited
through student acquaintances and did not test collective writing.
[Original paper scan](https://umwelt.se/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/Why-horoscopes-are-true-The-effects-of-specificity-on-acceptance-of-astrological-interpretations.pdf).

### Favourability is not a simple explanation

The publisher abstract of the 1980 study _Effects of “Favorability” and Type of
Assessment Device Upon Acceptance of General Personality Interpretations_ reports
that the favourability effect was attributable to higher base-rate accuracy in
the favourable descriptions. This cautions against treating flattering language
as an independently established mechanism: it may also describe common experiences
more accurately. The evidence concerns college students receiving personality
interpretations, not group statements.
[Primary article and abstract](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15327752jpa4401_6).

These findings motivate a writing strategy, not a scientifically validated recipe.
Neither paired tensions, descriptions of hidden effort nor an inclusive ending
has been independently tested here. Their effects should not be asserted merely
because they resemble familiar horoscope language.

## How the main assistant applied the research

| Choice in v2                                            | Intended operation                                                                          | Status                                                                 |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Unspecified things carried, postponed or troubling us   | Readers can supply experiences at personal or political scales without the text naming any. | Informed by general-feedback research; effect of these lines untested. |
| Change alongside protection; autonomy alongside company | Accommodates opposing inclinations without demanding a single consistent position.          | Writing hypothesis, not a separately demonstrated effect.              |
| Effort that other people do not see                     | Offers recognition of an unseen burden and a favourable self-understanding.                 | Writing hypothesis; may influence liking more than accuracy.           |
| Sometimes clear, sometimes unsure                       | Includes both decisive and uncertain readers without identifying which people are which.    | Writing hypothesis; broad applicability can also dilute meaning.       |
| Time, trust or someone taking a step with us            | Supplies several possible needs while leaving the reader to select what fits.               | Writing hypothesis; no evidence these are the cohort's actual needs.   |
| Different needs within an inclusive “we”                | Simulates room for diversity without representing any particular disagreement.              | Intentional control feature, not evidence of plural representation.    |

The opening proposes changing resignation to familiar conditions; the middle
holds conflicting wishes; the ending locates change partly in relationships.
This gives the passage the shape of a collective answer rather than a list of
personality traits. The text makes no claim that it heard or analysed the reader.
It includes neither participant quotations nor named political issues. This does
not establish that it is independent of everything its author has encountered.

## Provenance and freeze

The main assistant had already seen two human responses when writing v2. It
therefore **cannot be described as blind to those responses**, even though it was
written as a generic control rather than a synthesis of them. The author also
had the earlier synthetic experiments and the subagent research in context.

V1 was drafted by a subagent before the human responses arrived. V2 was written
later by the main assistant, because the creator requested that authorship.
These different provenance claims must not be conflated. V2 can be fixed before
future unseen contributions; use v1 if pre-exposure independence from the first
two responses is necessary. Neither version has been selected on participant
ratings or demonstrated to maximise recognition.
